Post this as the first comment the moment the launch is live. Edit the

Hi Product Hunt, I'm Richard, and I built Salsox on my own.

It started with a small, annoying problem. Most of the people I send documents to read them on a phone, often straight from a WhatsApp chat. I wanted signing to feel natural there. And once a contract was signed, there was always a separate invoice to send and chase; I wanted the payment to happen in the same step.

So Salsox does three things, and tries to do them well on a phone:

**Send in one screen.** Quick Send: upload any PDF, type who needs to sign, send. The signature and date fields are placed for you. The invitation goes by email, and you can share the same link on WhatsApp or by SMS.

**Sign with no account.** Your signer opens the link in their browser and signs one field at a time. No app, no sign-up.

**Get paid as they sign.** With Sign & Pay, a document can collect a payment as part of signing: by card through Stripe, or card and mobile money through Paystack. That's on the Business plan.

One small thing I'm proud of: if you typed someone's email wrong, you fix it on the document. The old link stops working, the right person gets a new one, and nothing has to be rebuilt.

**Pricing:** free for 3 documents a month, no card needed. Personal is $9 a month for unlimited documents, and Business is $19 a month with Sign & Pay and a 14-day free trial.

To be straight with you: Salsox is new, and it's one person. There will be rough edges, and I'd rather hear about them from you than guess. What would stop you from switching to it? What's missing for the documents you send?

## Why it is written this way

- **It opens with the problem, not the product.** Product Hunt readers decide in the first two lines whether to keep reading.
- **It has three bold headings.** They make the comment easy to skim and match gallery images 2, 3 and 4.
- **It says "new" and "one person" plainly.** It makes no claim that would need a number behind it: no "trusted by", no "thousands of documents", no awards.
- **It ends with two real questions.** Those get answers you can use, which "let me know what you think" does not.
