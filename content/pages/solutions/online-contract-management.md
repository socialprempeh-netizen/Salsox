## Signing is the part that stalls

For most small businesses, the hard part of contracts is not storing them; it is getting them signed. A contract emailed as an attachment waits for a printer, a scanner and a free afternoon. {site} turns that into a link the other party opens on their phone and signs in a couple of minutes, one field at a time, with no account to create. Contracts can go out by email, or be shared to WhatsApp or SMS from the document's page.

## Keeping contracts in one place

Every contract you send lives in one list, newest activity first. Each entry shows its title, status and how many of its signers have signed. Filter to see everything awaiting signatures, everything completed, anything declined or expired; search by title to find one contract among hundreds. Open any entry to see each signer's progress, download the signed PDF, or read its audit trail.

For a small team, that list often replaces the folder of scanned PDFs nobody can search and the spreadsheet of who signed what.

## Proving what was agreed

A contract is worth what you can prove about it. Each document keeps an [audit trail](/esignature-audit-trail) of when it was sent, opened and signed, by whom, from what device. On the Business plan, completed contracts are sealed with a certificate page, and anyone can confirm a signed copy has not changed using its verification code on the public verify page.

## Starting from a template

Most small businesses sign the same handful of contracts. The template library covers the common ones, such as a [service agreement](/templates/service-agreement), [freelance contract](/templates/freelance-contract), [mutual NDA](/templates/mutual-nda) and [residential lease](/templates/residential-lease). Fill in the details and the contract opens in the editor with every signature field already placed; on the Personal plan you can save your filled-in version to reuse. Templates are ready-made: {site} does not turn contracts you upload into reusable templates.

## Contract changes

When something changes after sending (a wrong email, an expired link), it is corrected on the same document without losing signatures already collected. When the contract itself must change, cancel it and send the revised version; the cancelled one stays in the list with its history.

## Your contracts, exportable

Records are yours. On any plan you can export every document, original and signed, with its audit trail, at any time, and you keep that right after cancelling.

## Where the limits are

Contract lifecycle management platforms do far more: they draft contracts from clause libraries, manage negotiation and redlines, track obligations and renewal dates, route approvals across departments, and connect to CRMs and ERPs. {site} does none of that. It does not remind you when a contract is due for renewal, and it does not store contracts that were signed somewhere else. If those are what you need, a CLM platform is the right tool; if you need contracts signed fast and kept organised, {site} is a simpler and cheaper fit.
