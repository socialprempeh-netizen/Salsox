## Where signNow is stronger

signNow covers more of the e-signature job than {site} does. Its site lists **unlimited templates**, **bulk sending**, **role-based signing order**, an **API** for embedding signatures in other software, native **mobile apps**, and integrations with tools such as Google Drive, Microsoft Word, Microsoft Teams, Salesforce and NetSuite. It also says plans include unlimited users, which suits a team that wants everyone sending from one subscription with their own login. If you send the same documents repeatedly, or need signing wired into a CRM or your own product, those features are the deciding factor, and {site} does not have them.

## Where {site} is different

**One flat, published price.** {site} lists its prices on its pricing page, per account, and paid plans never count documents. You can see what you will pay before you sign up, without a checkout flow.

**Built for signers on phones.** A {site} signer opens a link in any phone browser, is walked through one field at a time, and is done. There is nothing to install, and the link can be shared over WhatsApp or SMS from the document page as well as by email.

**Payments where cards are not the default.** Sign & Pay runs on Stripe or Paystack. With Paystack, a signer can pay a deposit by mobile money where it is supported, which matters for businesses whose clients rarely pay by card.

**Mistakes fixed in place.** If a signer's email address was wrong or their link expired, you correct or renew it on the same document. Fields and signatures already collected stay where they are.

**Free tools with no upload.** The free PDF tools sign, fill and add signatures to PDFs in the browser, without an account and without sending the file to a server.

## Switching from signNow

Download your completed documents and their audit trails from signNow before your plan ends: they remain your record of what was signed, and nothing needs to be signed again. Templates do not carry over, because {site} has none in the product. For each new agreement you upload the PDF and place fields in the editor, or let Quick Send add a signature and date for each signer. If most of your sends come from three or four templates, time one send in {site} before you switch, so you know the cost of the change in practice.

## Which should you choose?

Choose signNow if you rely on templates, bulk sending, an API or many users under one plan. Choose {site} if your documents vary, your signers are mostly on phones, you want a price you can read before you buy, or you need to collect a deposit by card or mobile money at the moment of signing. Both let signers sign without an account, so the quickest test is to send the same real document through each and ask the signer which was easier.
