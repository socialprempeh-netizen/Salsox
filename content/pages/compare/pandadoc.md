## Two different kinds of product

The clearest way to compare them is by where your document comes from.

**With PandaDoc, the document is made in PandaDoc.** You build proposals, quotes and contracts from templates and reusable content blocks, add pricing tables, send them, see when they are opened, and collect a signature at the end. It is designed for sales and operations teams whose documents follow a pattern and whose pipeline lives in a CRM.

**With {site}, the document already exists.** You wrote it in Word, Google Docs or your accountant's software, exported a PDF, and need it signed, perhaps with a deposit, by people who will read it on a phone. {site} handles that last stretch and nothing before it.

## Where PandaDoc is stronger

If you send many proposals or quotes that share structure, PandaDoc's templates, content library and pricing tables cut preparation time significantly, and its CRM integrations keep the document trail next to the deal. Team plans give each seat its own login and permissions. {site} offers none of this, and is not trying to.

## Where {site} is different

**Cost for small senders.** For one person or a small team sharing an account, a flat {site} plan costs less than per-seat pricing, and paid plans never count envelopes.

**Simplicity for signers.** {site} is built around signing on a phone: one field at a time, no account, links shareable over WhatsApp or SMS.

**Payments in African markets.** Sign & Pay works with Paystack as well as Stripe, so a signer can pay a deposit by card or, where Paystack supports it, by mobile money.

**Free tools and exports.** The free browser tools sign and fill PDFs without an account or upload, and every account can export all originals, signed PDFs and audit trails at any time.

## A fair way to test both

Take one real document you sent last month and send it through each tool to a colleague acting as the client. Note how long setup took, how the signing felt on their phone, and what you would have paid at your usual monthly volume. That one test usually settles the choice faster than any feature table.

## Which should you choose?

Choose PandaDoc if you need to *create* documents at scale, especially sales documents tied to a CRM. Choose {site} if your documents are already written and you need them signed, and paid, with as little friction for the signer as possible. Freelancers and small service businesses are usually in the second group; the [e-signatures for freelancers](/esignature-for/freelancers) page shows what that looks like in practice.
