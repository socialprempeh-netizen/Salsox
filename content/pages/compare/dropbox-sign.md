## At a glance

Dropbox Sign's figures were taken from its pricing page on **16 October 2026**, in USD (they had changed since our first check on 6 October). Prices change; check the source before you decide.

| | {site} | Dropbox Sign |
|---|---|---|
| One-sender plan | Personal: $9/month or $90/year | Essentials: $20/month, or $15/month billed yearly ($180/year) |
| Sending | No count on paid plans (fair use) | Unlimited signature requests on paid plans |
| Small team plan | No team seats today | Standard: $30 per user per month, or $25 billed yearly, 2 or more users |
| Templates | Ready-made library; your own PDFs are set up each time | Your own documents, on every plan |
| Bulk send | Not offered | Standard and above |
| Payment collection | Business plan (Stripe or Paystack) | Not listed on the pricing page |
| Signing without an account | Yes | Yes |

## Where Dropbox Sign is stronger

Dropbox Sign's pricing page lists **templates** and **signer attachments** on every plan and **bulk send** from Standard. If you send the same agreement of your own over and over, templates save setup time on each send; {site} offers ready-made templates but cannot yet save your own documents as templates. Dropbox Sign also has team plans with per-user administration, and it fits naturally if your files already live in Dropbox.

## Where {site} is different

**Price for a single sender.** At list prices, {site} Personal costs less than Dropbox Sign Essentials, and both send without counting.

**Getting paid with the signature.** {site}'s Business plan includes Sign & Pay: the signer pays a deposit or fee, by card through Stripe or by card and mobile money through Paystack, as part of signing. Payment collection is not listed on Dropbox Sign's pricing page.

**Signing order and approvals.** {site} Business supports sequential signing and an approver role, for documents that need an internal sign-off before a client sees them.

**WhatsApp and SMS sharing.** Every {site} signing link can be shared to WhatsApp or SMS from the document page, which helps where signers read messages before email.

**Fixes in place.** Wrong address or expired link: correct or renew it without rebuilding the document.

**Free tools.** {site}'s free PDF tools let anyone sign, fill and add signatures to PDFs in the browser without an account or upload.

## Switching from Dropbox Sign

Moving is mostly a matter of keeping your archive. Before your subscription ends, download your completed documents and their audit trails from Dropbox Sign; they remain valid records of what was signed, and nothing needs to be signed again. Your Dropbox Sign templates do not carry over, and {site} cannot yet store your own documents as templates: for each new agreement you start from one of its ready-made templates, or upload the PDF and either place fields in the editor or let Quick Send add a signature and date for each signer. If you sent the same few documents through templates, try one of them in {site} first and time it, so you know what the change costs you in practice. Signers notice little difference: they still receive a link and sign without an account.

## Which should you choose?

If your work revolves around a handful of standard agreements sent many times, Dropbox Sign's templates and bulk send will save more time than anything {site} offers today. If you send varied, one-off documents, want to collect a deposit at signing, or sign with people who live on their phones, {site} covers that for less. Either way, both let signers sign without an account, so you can try a real document with each before committing.
