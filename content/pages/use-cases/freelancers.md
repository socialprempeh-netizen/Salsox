## Why the contract step loses freelancers work

The pattern is familiar. A call goes well, the client says yes, you promise to send the contract. You send a PDF, they mean to print and sign it, a week passes, and the project start slips or the client cools off. Then the deposit invoice goes out separately and starts its own wait.

Every step between agreement and payment is a chance for the project to stall. E-signatures cut out the printing and scanning; Sign & Pay cuts out the separate invoice.

## Sending a contract the way clients actually respond

1. **Write the contract as you normally do**, and export it as a PDF.
2. **Upload it and add your client.** For a simple contract, Quick Send places a signature and date field for them automatically.
3. **Add the deposit** (Business plan): an amount and a currency, paid by the client as the last step of signing.
4. **Send, and share the link where they will see it.** Email goes automatically; if your client lives on WhatsApp, share the same link there from the document's page.
5. **Countersign.** If you want to sign after the client, make signing sequential so you are invited once they have finished.

When it is done, both of you receive the signed PDF, the deposit is in your account, and you can start work.

## Small things that matter for a one-person business

- **No envelope counting.** Paid plans have no limit to watch, so a busy month does not cost extra.
- **Flat price.** $9 a month for unlimited signing, or $19 for Sign & Pay and signing order, instead of per-seat pricing built for teams.
- **Mistakes are cheap.** Sent the contract to the client's old email? Correct the address; the old link stops working.
- **Proof if it is ever needed.** Each document has an audit trail of views and signatures, and the signed PDF has a verification code the client, or anyone, can check.
- **Your records follow you.** Export every contract and its audit trail as a ZIP for your accountant or your own backup.
