## Paper in the waiting room

A patient arrives, takes a clipboard, fills in a form, signs the consent sheet, and someone scans or files it later. The scan is crooked, the handwriting unclear, and the form is somewhere other than the patient's record. Multiply by a full clinic day and the paperwork alone needs a person.

## Consent, recorded properly

The [patient consent form](/templates/patient-consent-form) is built around what informed consent requires: the procedure and why it is recommended, its main risks, the alternatives including no treatment, confirmation that the patient's questions were answered, and the fee. The patient initials the risks section and signs; the clinician signs a statement that they explained it. Each signature is time-stamped in the audit trail.

Send the link to the patient's phone before the appointment so they can read it at home, or open it on a clinic tablet at the front desk. Either way, the signed PDF comes back ready to attach to the patient's record.

## Treatment paid over time

For treatment a patient pays for in instalments, the [payment plan agreement](/templates/payment-plan-agreement) sets the schedule and what happens if a payment is missed. On the Business plan, the first instalment can be paid as part of signing, by card or by mobile money through Paystack where it is supported.

## Staff and suppliers

Clinics are employers and buyers too. Offer letters, employment contracts and an [employee confidentiality agreement](/templates/employee-nda) protect patient information from the first day. Regular supplies of consumables run better under a [supplier agreement](/templates/supplier-agreement) with purchase orders sent against it.

## What to check first

Health data is regulated, and the rules differ widely. In the United States, providers handling protected health information need a business associate agreement with every vendor that touches it, and {site} does not offer one, so it is not the right tool there for patient records. Elsewhere, check your national data protection and health privacy rules, and your regulator's guidance on consent records. Use the clinic's own consent wording for high-risk procedures.

## Reception, step by step

A workable front-desk routine looks like this. When an appointment is booked, reception starts the consent form from the template, fills in the procedure, risks and fee for that treatment, and adds the patient and the clinician as signers. The patient gets the link the evening before and reads it without being rushed. At the appointment the clinician goes through it with them, answers questions, and both sign: the patient on their own phone or the tablet, the clinician on theirs. The signed PDF is saved to the record before the patient leaves the chair.

## Day to day

Each document's page shows whether the patient has opened and signed it. Unsigned forms are reminded automatically, and corrected email addresses get a fresh link without losing anything already signed.
