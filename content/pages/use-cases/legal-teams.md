## Legal as a bottleneck

In a small or growing company, every NDA, supplier contract and policy acknowledgement can end up in one lawyer's inbox. Most are standard: the same NDA, the same supplier terms, the same handbook. Reviewing each by hand does not make the company safer; it makes deals slower and teaches colleagues to work around legal.

## Approved templates, filled in by the business

The scalable answer is a small set of approved templates the business can use without asking. In {site}, a template's document text stays fixed; a colleague who uses it types the blanks (names, dates, amounts) but not the clauses, and can save their filled-in version to reuse. Start with the documents that arrive most often:

- a [mutual NDA](/templates/mutual-nda) and a [one-way NDA](/templates/one-way-nda);
- a [service agreement](/templates/service-agreement) for what the company sells;
- a [supplier agreement](/templates/supplier-agreement) and [purchase order](/templates/purchase-order) for what it buys;
- an [employment contract](/templates/employment-contract) and [employee confidentiality agreement](/templates/employee-nda) with HR.

Anything that departs from the template comes to legal; everything else goes straight out.

## Approvals where they matter

Some documents should not reach a counterparty without legal's sign-off: a non-standard contract, a large sponsorship, a partnership. On the Business plan, legal can be added as an approver ahead of the counterparty, with signing set to sequential, so the other side is invited only once approval is in. The audit trail records the approval as well as the signatures.

## Evidence and records

Every completed document comes back as a PDF with an audit trail of each signer, the time and the device. On the Business plan the PDF is sealed with a certificate page, and its verification code can be checked on the public verify page to show the document has not changed. All documents and audit trails can be exported at any time, on any plan, which matters for audits, disputes and due diligence.

## Knowing the limits

Simple electronic signatures are valid for most commercial contracts in most countries, but some documents (deeds, certain property transfers, witnessed or notarised documents) need more. {site} does not provide witnessing, notarisation or qualified electronic signatures. Keep a short list of document types that need a different process, and share it with the business along with the templates.

## Counterparties who want their own paper

Larger customers and suppliers will often insist on their own contract. When they do, upload their PDF, place signature and date fields for each party in the editor, and send it like any other document. The audit trail and the signed copy are the same, so the company keeps one record of every agreement however it was drafted, and legal can find any of them later from the documents list.

## Policies and acknowledgements

When a policy changes, every employee should confirm they have read it. The [employee handbook acknowledgment](/templates/employee-handbook-acknowledgment) records which version each person received, and the document list shows who has not yet signed.
