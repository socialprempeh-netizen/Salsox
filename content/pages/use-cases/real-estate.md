## The documents, and how they move

Real estate paperwork tends to share three traits: it involves several parties, it is time-sensitive, and money changes hands at or near signing. Each document type uses those differently.

**Tenancy and lease agreements.** Usually a tenant, a landlord and often an agent. The tenant signs first, the landlord countersigns, and a deposit or first month's rent is due. With {site}'s Business plan, the lease goes to the tenant first, collects the deposit as part of their signing, and only then goes to the landlord: one document instead of a lease, an invoice and a chase.

**Offers and reservation forms.** Speed matters more than anything. Buyers make offers from wherever they are, so a signing link sent by WhatsApp and completed on a phone in two minutes can be the difference between securing a property and missing it.

**Inspection, inventory and move-in reports.** Several pages, each to be acknowledged. Initials on every page with a signature at the end are set up once in the editor.

**Renewals and notices.** Short documents with a deadline. Quick Send handles them: upload, enter the tenant's email, send, and reminders go out every three days until it is signed.

**Management agreements** between owners and agents. Signed once, referred to for years, so a reliable archive matters more than speed.

## What makes property signing go wrong, and the fix

- **A tenant gives the wrong email.** Correct it on the document's page; the old link stops working and the right person gets a new one.
- **An offer sits unsigned while the deadline passes.** Set a short link expiry for offers, so a stale offer cannot be signed late. An expired document can be renewed if both sides still want it.
- **Nobody can find last year's lease.** Every signed document stays in your account, and the full archive exports as a ZIP of PDFs and audit trails.
- **Someone disputes a signature.** Each document's audit trail records views and signatures with time, IP address and browser, and the signed PDF carries a verification code anyone can check.

## Where the law still asks for more

Electronic signatures are widely accepted for leases and the contracts around a sale. Transfers of land are different: many jurisdictions require deeds to be signed in ink, witnessed, or executed before a notary, and some registries accept only specific qualified electronic signatures. {site} provides simple electronic signatures with an audit trail, not qualified signatures. Use it for the agreements, and follow the land registry's rules for the deed.
