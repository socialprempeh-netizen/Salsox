## After the contract is signed

Sales gets the first signature. Everything after it belongs to the people who look after the customer: the onboarding details that let the work start, the statement of work when the customer wants more, the renewal, the payment plan when a customer falls behind, the sign-off when a job is done. A slow signature at any of these points delays revenue or sours the relationship.

## Onboarding that starts on day one

The [client onboarding form](/templates/client-onboarding-form) collects what the team needs in one signed document: legal and billing details, the main and billing contacts, the customer's goals, the access you need, and their agreement to your terms and privacy notice. Writing the goals down at the start gives the account manager something concrete to report against at the first review.

## Expansions without a new contract

When a customer wants more, the master terms usually stay the same; only the scope and price change. A [statement of work](/templates/statement-of-work) defines the new project with milestones, acceptance and fees under the existing agreement, and the customer signs it like any other document. For work billed monthly, a [retainer agreement](/templates/retainer-agreement) sets the included hours.

## Field service and sign-offs

Teams that send technicians to customers close each visit with a [work order](/templates/work-order): the job, labour and parts, and the customer's signature that it is done and the charges are agreed. Signed on site, it becomes an invoice the same day.

## When payments slip

A good customer with a cash-flow problem is worth keeping. A [payment plan agreement](/templates/payment-plan-agreement) records the balance, the instalments and what happens if one is missed. On the Business plan, the first instalment is paid as the customer signs, by card or by mobile money through Paystack where it is supported.

## Remote working terms

Customer success teams are often distributed; a [remote work agreement](/templates/remote-work-agreement) sets core hours, response times and data-security rules for handling customer information at home.

## Renewals on record

Renewal is the quiet risk in an account: terms change slightly, a discount is agreed on a call, and a year later nobody remembers what was said. Send the renewal terms as a short document, signed by the customer, recording the new period, the price and anything that changed. It takes a minute to prepare from a saved template, and it means the next renewal starts from a signed record rather than from memory or an old email thread.

## One view of what is waiting

The documents list shows every onboarding form, SOW and sign-off and whether it has been signed. Reminders go out automatically, wrong email addresses can be corrected without losing signatures, and each completed document has an audit trail you can share if a customer later asks what was agreed.
