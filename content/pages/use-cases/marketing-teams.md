## Paperwork marketing owns

Legal reviews the big contracts, but day-to-day campaign paperwork usually sits with the marketing team: the release from a customer who appears in a testimonial video, the agreement with a freelance photographer, the sponsor for the conference stand, the NDA with the agency before a launch brief. These are small documents with real consequences: an ad pulled because nobody can find the release, or a sponsor who believed they had bought exclusivity.

## Releases at the shoot

The [photo release form](/templates/photo-release-form) names the shoot, lists where the material may appear (website, social, print, in-store), sets a time limit, states any compensation and explains how consent can be withdrawn. Send it to each person's phone on the day; they sign before the camera starts, and the signed copy is on file before the edit begins.

## Sponsorships and partnerships

Event sponsorships go wrong when benefits are loose. The [sponsorship agreement](/templates/sponsorship-agreement) lists each deliverable (logo placements, stage time, passes, the post-event report) along with category exclusivity and what happens if the event moves. For co-marketing with another company, a [memorandum of understanding](/templates/memorandum-of-understanding) records the plan and makes confidentiality and logo use binding even where the rest is not.

## Agencies and freelancers

Outside help needs clear terms: a [freelance contract](/templates/freelance-contract) for designers and writers, with revisions and ownership settled, or a [retainer](/templates/retainer-agreement) for ongoing work. Sensitive launches start with a [one-way NDA](/templates/one-way-nda) before the brief goes out.

## Referral and affiliate partners

When partners send customers your way, a [referral agreement](/templates/referral-agreement) defines a qualified referral, the commission and how disputes over credit are settled, so partner programmes do not end in arguments.

## Influencers and creators

Paid partnerships with creators need the same clarity as a sponsorship, in reverse: what the creator will post, where and when, how the content may be reused, what disclosure they must include, and how they are paid. Adapt the [freelance contract](/templates/freelance-contract) with those deliverables and the platform's disclosure rules, and pair it with a release for any material the brand will reuse in its own ads. Signed before the brief goes out, it avoids the post that goes live without the label regulators expect.

## Budget sign-off

Inside the company, a campaign often needs a budget approval before contracts go out. On the Business plan, a manager can be added as an approver ahead of the agency or sponsor, so nothing is sent before the money is agreed.

## Keeping track

Signed PDFs come back with an audit trail, so when someone asks whether the person in the hero image agreed to be there, the answer is a search away.
