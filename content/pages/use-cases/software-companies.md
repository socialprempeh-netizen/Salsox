## The documents a small software company signs

Early-stage software companies rarely have a contracts team, yet the paperwork arrives from every direction at once. Sales needs NDAs before deep-dive demos and order forms or statements of work to close. Engineering brings in contractors who need agreements that assign the code they write. Hiring means offer letters and employment contracts, often across borders. Each of these sits in someone's inbox until a person with a pen gets to it, and each delay costs either revenue or a candidate.

## Closing customers

The moment a buyer says yes is the moment to send the paper. The [sales proposal template](/templates/sales-proposal) recaps the buyer's situation, prices the plan and one-off costs in a table, and ends with an order block; the [statement of work template](/templates/statement-of-work) does the same for a defined project, milestone by milestone. Either opens in the editor with the buyer's signature already placed, so the gap between "send it over" and "sent" is a couple of minutes.

For implementation fees or a first period paid upfront, the Business plan's Sign & Pay collects the payment as part of signing. The money goes to your Stripe or Paystack account, not through a separate invoice chase.

## Contractors and code ownership

Contract developers and designers are common in small software teams, and the clause that matters most is ownership. The [independent contractor agreement](/templates/independent-contractor-agreement) assigns work product to the company once paid, keeps the contractor genuinely independent, and sets invoicing terms. Pair it with an NDA when the contractor will see customer data or unreleased plans.

## Hiring

Offers in tech move fast and candidates compare them on their phones. The [job offer letter template](/templates/job-offer-letter) states salary, benefits, start date and probation in plain terms, and the candidate can accept from wherever they are. The employment contract follows with the full terms.

## What {site} does not do

Honesty saves you a migration later. {site} has no public API, no CRM integration and no bulk send. If your sales process lives inside Salesforce or HubSpot and you need contracts generated from deal data, a larger platform fits better. If you want a fast, inexpensive way to get a growing number of documents signed by people who are rarely at a desk, {site} is built for that, at a flat price per account rather than per seat.

## A workable setup

Most small software teams settle on three habits: save the NDA and order form as their own templates with the company details filled in, send everything from the dashboard so each document's status is visible, and let automatic reminders chase anyone who has not signed. Every completed document comes back with a signed PDF and an audit trail of who signed, when and from which device.
