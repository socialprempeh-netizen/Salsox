Every industry signs things, but not the same things, and not under the same constraints. Here is how e-signatures fit six common sectors: the typical documents, what tends to go wrong, and where the law still asks for more than a click.

*General information, not legal advice. Requirements differ by country and change over time.*

## Property and real estate

**Typical documents:** tenancy agreements, offers, reservation forms, inventories, renewals, management agreements.

**What matters:** several parties, deadlines and money. A lease is usually signed by the tenant, then the landlord, with a deposit due. Being able to collect the deposit as part of signing, and to set the order, saves a round of chasing.

**Watch out for:** deeds and transfers of land, which in many jurisdictions require ink, witnesses or a notary. E-sign the agreements around a sale; follow the registry's rules for the deed. More in [e-signatures for real estate](/esignature-for/real-estate).

## HR and employment

**Typical documents:** offer letters, employment contracts, onboarding forms, policy acknowledgements, exit agreements.

**What matters:** speed for offers, internal approval before a candidate sees terms, and a clean record of who acknowledged which policy.

**Watch out for:** personal data. Make sure only the intended person can open each document, and that you can export and retain records according to your obligations. More in [e-signatures for HR](/esignature-for/hr).

## Freelancers and consultants

**Typical documents:** client contracts, statements of work, NDAs, change requests.

**What matters:** turning a verbal yes into a signed contract and a paid deposit before the client's enthusiasm fades. Signing on a phone and paying in the same step does most of the work.

**Watch out for:** sending a contract that does not cover scope, payment terms and ownership of work clearly. E-signatures make it fast to agree; they do not make a weak contract stronger. More in [e-signatures for freelancers](/esignature-for/freelancers).

## Healthcare and wellness

**Typical documents:** consent forms, intake questionnaires, treatment agreements, policy acknowledgements.

**What matters:** patients and clients signing before or at an appointment, often on a phone or tablet.

**Watch out for:** health data rules. In the US, HIPAA applies to covered entities and their business associates; in the EU, health data is a special category under GDPR. Check that a tool meets your obligations, including any agreements your regulator requires, before collecting health information through it. {site} is not marketed as a HIPAA-compliant service.

## Education and training

**Typical documents:** enrolment forms, parental consent, trip permissions, tutor agreements.

**What matters:** reaching parents and guardians who may not check email, and collecting fees at enrolment.

**Watch out for:** consent for minors, which must come from someone with authority to give it. Make sure the signing link goes to the parent or guardian, not the student.

## Trades and home services

**Typical documents:** quotes, work orders, completion sign-offs, warranty terms.

**What matters:** signing on site, on the customer's phone, and taking a deposit before ordering materials.

**Watch out for:** quotes signed weeks after prices changed. Give quote links a short expiry so an old price cannot be accepted late.

## What every industry has in common

Whatever the sector, three habits make e-signatures hold up: send each person their own link rather than a shared one; keep the audit trail, not just the signed PDF; and know which of your documents the law still wants in ink. The guide to [whether electronic signatures are legal](/blog/are-electronic-signatures-legal) covers the last point.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which industries use e-signatures the most?

Property, financial services, staffing and HR, insurance, and professional services are among the heaviest users, because their work runs on signed agreements with people outside the business.

### Can healthcare consent forms be signed electronically?

Often yes, but health data carries extra legal duties (such as HIPAA in the US or GDPR's special categories in the EU), and some consents have specific formal requirements. Check both before choosing a tool.

### Do e-signatures work for customers without email?
