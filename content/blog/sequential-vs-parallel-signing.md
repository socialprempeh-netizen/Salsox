When a document needs more than one signature, you have to decide whether everyone gets it at once or one after another. It sounds like a detail, but it changes how fast the document comes back and, sometimes, who is bound to what and when. This guide explains the two approaches, where an approver fits in, and which order suits common documents.

## Parallel signing: everyone at once

In **parallel** signing, every signer is invited at the same time and signs whenever they like, in any order. The document is complete when the last person signs.

**Use it when** the order does not matter: two co-founders signing a shareholders' agreement, both tenants signing a lease, three parents signing a consent form. It is the fastest option because nobody waits for anybody else, and it is the default in most e-signature tools, including Salsox.

## Sequential signing: one after another

In **sequential** signing, also called signing order or routing order, the document goes to one person at a time. The second signer is invited only when the first has finished, and so on.

**Use it when** the order changes what people see or agree to:

- **Approval before sending.** A manager or legal reviewer should check a contract before the client sees it.
- **Offer before acceptance.** You want the other side to sign first, so you are not bound until they have agreed, then countersign.
- **Information that builds up.** The first person fills in details, such as a start date or delivery address, that the next person needs to see before signing.

The cost is time: each person waits for the one before. A signer who is on holiday holds up everyone after them, so keep sequences short and set reminders.

## Where approvers fit

Not everyone who needs to agree to a document has to *sign* it. An **approver** confirms the document is right, often someone inside your own business, without being a party to it. Placing an approver first in a sequence means the client never receives a document that has not been checked.

In Salsox you set this up in the editor's recipients step: give each person a role (signs, approves, views or gets a copy), put them in order with the arrows, and tick **Sign in order (one after another)**:

## Common signing orders

| Document | Suggested order |
|---|---|
| Client contract or quote | Parallel if only the client signs; otherwise client first, you countersign |
| Contract needing internal sign-off | Approver, then client, then you |
| Employment offer | Hiring manager approves, candidate signs, HR or a director countersigns |
| Lease with two tenants | Both tenants in parallel, landlord last |
| Sales agreement with a deposit | Buyer first (paying the deposit at signing), seller countersigns |
| Consent or acknowledgement form | Parallel: each person signs their own copy |

## Why "who signs first" can matter legally

In many legal systems, a signed contract is an offer and acceptance in writing. If you sign first, you may have made a binding offer the other side can accept by signing, even after circumstances change. Letting the other party sign first, then countersigning, means you are not bound until you have seen their signature and checked nothing was altered. For high-value agreements, ask your lawyer which order they prefer.

## Tips for multi-signer documents

- **Keep sequences short.** Every step adds waiting time; only order the people who truly need it.
- **Tell people they are in a sequence.** A line in the message such as "Efua will review this first, then you'll receive your link" prevents confusion.
- **Watch the status page.** The document's page shows who has signed and who is next; reminders go out automatically every three days.
- **Fix mistakes without restarting.** A wrong email address can be corrected in place; the old link stops working.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is sequential signing?

Sequential signing sends a document to one signer at a time, in a set order. Each person is invited only after the previous one has finished.

### What is parallel signing?

Parallel signing invites every signer at once, and they sign in any order. The document completes when the last one signs.

### Which is faster?

Parallel, because nobody waits for anyone else. Use sequential only when the order matters, for example an approval before the client sees the document.

### Who should sign a contract first?

Usually the party receiving the offer, so the party who drafted it is not bound until the other side has agreed. The drafter countersigns last.
