A sales agreement, also called a sales contract or purchase agreement, records the sale of goods in writing: what is sold, for how much, and on what terms. For a small cash sale across a counter, nobody writes one. For a larger order, made-to-order goods or a sale paid in instalments, it is what stops a misunderstanding about the specification, the delivery date or who pays for damage in transit from becoming a dispute.

*This article is general information, not legal advice.*

## 1. The parties

Use full legal names and addresses. If a business is buying or selling, name the business, not just the person signing for it: "Osei Boutique Hotel Ltd", not "Kwame". If the buyer is a consumer rather than a business, consumer protection law in your country may give them rights the agreement cannot remove.

## 2. A precise description of the goods

Most sales disputes are about whether the buyer received what they agreed to buy. Describe the goods closely enough that a stranger could check the delivery against the agreement:

- **What:** model numbers, materials, sizes, colours, grades.
- **How many:** quantities, and how partial deliveries are handled.
- **Condition:** new, refurbished or used, and any known defects.

Attach a specification, drawing or photo if words are not enough, and say in the agreement that it forms part of it.

## 3. Price, deposit and payment terms

State the unit price, the total, and whether tax and delivery are included. Then say how and when payment is made:

- **A deposit** on signing, especially for made-to-order goods. It covers your costs if the buyer cancels and shows the buyer is committed. Our guide to [asking a client for a deposit](/blog/how-to-ask-a-client-for-a-deposit) covers how much to ask for and how to word it.
- **The balance:** on delivery, within a number of days of delivery, or in instalments with dates.
- **Late payment:** what happens if the balance is not paid on time.

## 4. Delivery

Give the delivery address, the date or window, who arranges transport and who pays for it. Say what happens if delivery is late: a new date agreed in writing is better than silence. For international sales, traders often use **Incoterms**, standard terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce that define who handles and pays for each stage of shipping.

## 5. Ownership and risk

These are two different things, and a good sales agreement treats them separately:

- **Risk** is who bears the loss if the goods are damaged or lost. It usually passes on delivery.
- **Ownership** (title) is who owns the goods. Sellers often keep ownership until they are paid in full, a "retention of title" clause, so unpaid goods can be recovered.

## 6. Inspection and acceptance

Give the buyer a short period after delivery to inspect the goods and report shortages or damage in writing. After that, the goods are treated as accepted, except for hidden defects that could not reasonably have been found. This protects both sides: the buyer gets time to check, and the seller is not facing complaints about a delivery months later.

## 7. Warranty and faulty goods

Say how long the goods are warranted, what is covered, and what the seller will do: repair, replace or refund. Exclude what is fair to exclude, such as misuse and normal wear. Statutory rights, especially for consumers, apply whatever the contract says.

## 8. Cancellation

Can the buyer cancel, and until when? A common approach: free cancellation before the goods are made or dispatched, with the seller keeping reasonable costs from the deposit; after dispatch, only by agreement.

## 9. The general terms

- **Entire agreement:** the written agreement replaces earlier conversations.
- **Changes:** only in writing, agreed by both.
- **Governing law:** which country's (or state's) law applies.

## 10. Signatures

Both parties sign. Signing electronically is fine for ordinary sales of goods in most countries, under laws such as the US ESIGN Act and the EU's eIDAS Regulation, and it gives a better record than a scanned signature: each signer's consent, the time they signed and the signed PDF itself.

## Checklist before you send

- Parties named in full.
- Goods described so a stranger could check the delivery.
- Price, deposit and balance terms clear.
- Delivery date, address and cost stated.
- Ownership and risk addressed.
- Inspection period, warranty and cancellation set out.
- Governing law chosen.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a sales agreement include?

The parties, a precise description and quantity of the goods, the price with any deposit and payment terms, delivery details, when ownership and risk pass, an inspection period, the warranty and remedy for faulty goods, cancellation terms, the governing law and both signatures.

### Is a sales agreement the same as a sales contract?

Yes. The terms are used interchangeably for a written agreement to sell goods. A purchase agreement is the same document seen from the buyer's side.

### Does a sales agreement need to be notarised?

Ordinary sales of goods do not. Sales of land and some other assets have formal requirements that vary by country, so check before using a general template for those.

### Can a sales agreement be signed electronically?

Yes, in most countries, under laws such as the US ESIGN Act and the EU's eIDAS Regulation. Both parties sign from their own link, and the signed PDF keeps a record of who signed and when.
