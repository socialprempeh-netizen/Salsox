Everyone sends a document with a mistake eventually: the wrong email address, a deadline that passes before the signer gets to it, a missing person, or the wrong PDF altogether. Most of these can be fixed in place without starting again. One cannot, and knowing which is which saves a lot of awkward emails.

## What you can fix, and what you can't

| Mistake | Fix in place? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong email address | Yes | Edit the recipient; the old link stops working |
| Wrong name or phone number | Yes | Edit the recipient |
| Link expired before they signed | Yes | Extend the links |
| Email didn't arrive | Yes | Resend, or share the link by WhatsApp or SMS |
| Wrong or outdated document | No | Cancel, then send the corrected document |
| Missing signer or wrong fields | No | Cancel, then send again with the right setup |

The rule behind the table: **who receives the document can change; what they are signing cannot.** Once a document is sent, its content and fields are fixed, because a signature only means something if the signer and the sender saw the same document.

## Wrong email address

This is the most common mistake and the easiest to fix. On the document's page, choose **Edit** beside the recipient, correct the address and save.

[![The Salsox Edit recipient dialog: name, email and phone, with the note that changing the email replaces their link and keeps their fields](/blog/screens/edit-recipient.png)](/blog/screens/edit-recipient.png)

Two things happen. The **old link stops working immediately**, so whoever received it by mistake cannot sign. And the **corrected address gets a fresh link**, with the same fields assigned to them as before. The change is recorded in the audit trail.

If the wrong address belonged to a real person outside your business, a short note to them is courteous: "Sorry, that was sent to you by mistake; the link no longer works."

## The link expired

Signing links should expire, so an old offer cannot be signed months later. When someone runs out of time, **extend the links** from the document's page. Every unsigned recipient gets more time, and a document that has already expired comes back to life. **Signatures already given stay in place**, so nobody has to sign twice. Pick an expiry that matches the document next time: thirty days for most contracts, less for time-limited quotes.

## The email didn't arrive

Check the address first: a typo means editing the recipient, not resending. If the address is right, **resend** the invitation, ask the person to check their spam folder, or skip email altogether and share their link from the **Share** menu by WhatsApp, SMS or your own email app. [Sending documents for signature on WhatsApp](/blog/send-documents-for-signature-on-whatsapp) explains how.

## The wrong document

If you attached the wrong PDF, or the right PDF with a mistake in it, the document must be replaced:

1. **Cancel the document** from its page straight away. Every signing link stops working, so nobody signs the wrong version.
2. **Tell the recipients** in a line: "Please ignore the agreement I sent this morning; a corrected version follows."
3. **Send the corrected PDF** as a new document.

If someone had already signed the wrong version, their signature does not carry over. That is deliberate: a signature applies to the exact document the person saw. Keep the cancelled document and its audit trail; it shows what happened and when.

## A missing signer or wrong fields

Recipients and fields are set when the document is sent, so adding a forgotten signer or moving a misplaced signature box also means cancelling and sending again. To avoid it, check the **Send** step before sending: every signer listed, each with their own fields, and signing order set if it matters ([sequential vs parallel signing](/blog/sequential-vs-parallel-signing) explains when it does).

## When the signer finds the mistake

Sometimes the signer spots the problem: a wrong date, a misspelt name, a clause you agreed to remove. A well-designed signing page lets them **decline with a reason** rather than sign something wrong. You will see the reason on the document's page, fix the document, and send the corrected version. Our guide on [what to do when someone won't sign](/blog/what-to-do-when-someone-wont-sign) shows what that looks like.

## Habits that prevent most mistakes

- **Copy email addresses** from a previous email rather than typing them.
- **Name files clearly** so the right PDF is the obvious one to attach.
- **Read the Send step** once before pressing send.
- **Send to yourself first** when you set up an important document for the first time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I edit a document after sending it for signature?

You can correct who it goes to: a recipient's name, email or phone. You cannot change the document's content once it has been sent; for that, cancel it and send a corrected version.

### What happens to the old link when I correct an email address?

In Salsox it stops working immediately, and the corrected address receives a new link. The recipient's fields stay as they were.

### Can I extend the deadline on a document?

Yes. Extending the links gives every unsigned recipient more time, and revives a document that has already expired. Signatures already given stay.

### What if someone has already signed the wrong version?

Cancel the document, send the corrected version to everyone, and keep the cancelled one for your records. A signature on one version never carries over to another.
