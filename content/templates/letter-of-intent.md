about: |
  A letter of intent records that two parties mean to do a deal and the main terms they have in mind, before the time and cost of a full contract. It is common for company and property purchases, joint ventures, large supply contracts and senior hires. It lets both sides check they agree on the essentials and gives advisers a brief to draft from.

  The most important thing an LOI does is say which parts are binding. This template keeps the commercial terms, the price, the structure and the conditions, explicitly not binding, so either side can still walk away during due diligence. It makes three parts binding: exclusivity for a limited period, confidentiality, and each side paying its own costs.

  Write the commercial terms in as much detail as you have agreed. A vague LOI saves time now and costs it later, when the gap between what each side thought they agreed shows up in the full contract.
faq:

    a: "Only the parts it says are binding. This template makes exclusivity, confidentiality and costs binding, and states clearly that the deal terms are not, until a full agreement is signed."
  - q: "What is exclusivity in an LOI?"
    a: "A promise by the seller not to negotiate with anyone else for a set period, giving the buyer time to do due diligence without being outbid. Keep the period realistic for the work involved."
  - q: "Is an LOI the same as a memorandum of understanding?"
    a: "They are close. An LOI is usually one party proposing terms to another; an MOU often records a shared understanding between partners. Both separate binding from non-binding terms."

# Letter of Intent

[Letter date]

To: [Recipient name], [Recipient company]

**Re: Proposed [Proposed transaction]**

This letter sets out the main terms on which [Proposer company] (the "Proposer") proposes to proceed with [Proposed transaction] (the "Transaction").

## Part A: proposed terms (not binding)

**1. Price.** [Price].

**2. Structure of payment.** [Payment structure].

**3. Conditions.** The Transaction is conditional on [Key conditions].

**4. Due diligence.** The Proposer expects to complete its due diligence within [Due diligence period] of countersignature, and the recipient will give reasonable access to information, premises and people for that purpose.

**5. Timetable.** Both parties will aim to sign a full agreement and complete the Transaction by [Target completion].

The terms in Part A are a statement of current intention only. They do not create any legal obligation. No party is bound to proceed with the Transaction unless and until a full written agreement is signed.

## Part B: binding terms

**6. Exclusivity.** For [Exclusivity period], the recipient will not solicit, encourage or negotiate any offer from anyone else for the same or a similar transaction, and will tell the Proposer promptly of any approach received.

**7. Confidentiality.** Each party will keep the existence and terms of this letter, and all information received in connection with the Transaction, confidential, except as needed to share with its advisers or as required by law.

**8. Costs.** Each party pays its own costs in connection with the Transaction, whether or not it proceeds.

**9. Governing law.** This letter is governed by the law of [Governing law].

Please confirm your agreement by countersigning below.

Yours sincerely,

[[signature:proposer]]
[Proposer signatory], for [Proposer company]
