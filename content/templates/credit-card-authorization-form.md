about: |
  A credit card authorization form records a customer's permission for a business to charge their card, either once or on a schedule. It is used where the customer is not present at the time of the charge: memberships, retainers, deposits, and phone or email orders.

  This form states the amount, what it is for and when it will be charged, and how the cardholder can cancel. A signed authorization is useful evidence if the cardholder later disputes a charge, though card network rules decide every chargeback.

  Card data is sensitive. Do not collect the full card number or security code on a form that is emailed or stored. The safest approach is to take the card through your payment provider's secure page and use this form for the authorization only; the template asks for the last four digits, not the full number, for that reason.
