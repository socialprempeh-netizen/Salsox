about: |
  Informed consent is a conversation between clinician and patient; the form is its record. It should show that the patient was told what the procedure is, why it is recommended, its main risks, the alternatives including doing nothing, and that their questions were answered before they agreed.

  This template is structured around those elements, so a clinic can fill in the specifics for each procedure. It also covers the practical points patients care about: the fee, and how their records and personal information are handled, which health privacy laws in most countries require clinics to explain. The patient initials the risks and alternatives section, and both patient and clinician sign.

  Requirements differ by country, profession and procedure; check your regulator's guidance, and use the clinic's own wording for high-risk procedures. Patients can sign on a tablet in the waiting room through {site}.

# Consent to Treatment

**Clinic:** [Clinic name]
**Patient:** [Patient name], born [Date of birth]
**Clinician:** [Clinician name]

## 1. Proposed treatment

[Procedure].

Reason: [Reason].

## 2. Risks and alternatives

The main risks explained to me are [Risks].

The alternatives explained to me are [Alternatives].

Patient initials: [[initials:patient]]

## 3. Questions and understanding

I have had the chance to ask questions, and they were answered to my satisfaction. I understand that I can change my mind and withdraw consent at any time before or during treatment, and that unexpected findings during treatment will be discussed with me where possible.

## 4. Fees

The fee for this treatment is [Fee]. Additional treatment will only be carried out after a new discussion and agreement.

## 5. Records and privacy

The clinic will keep my records confidential and securely, use them for my care and as the law requires, and share them only with my consent or where the law allows.

## Patient consent

I consent to the treatment described above.

Name: [[name:patient]]
