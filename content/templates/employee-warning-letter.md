about: |
  A written warning is part of a fair disciplinary or performance process: it tells the employee clearly what the problem is, what needs to change, and what happens if it does not, after they have had a chance to explain at a meeting. Done well, it is the step that most often fixes the problem.

  This template records the facts with dates, the employee's explanation from the meeting, the standard expected, and the support the employer will give, which matters both for fairness and for whether the change is realistic. It states the level of warning, how long it stays active, what the next step would be, and how to appeal.

  The employee signs only to acknowledge receipt, not agreement, and the letter says so. Many countries' laws or codes of practice set out what a fair disciplinary process involves, such as a meeting, the right to be accompanied and the right to appeal; follow your own policy and the law where the employee works.

# Written Warning

[Letter date]

Dear [Employee name],

**Re: [Warning level]**

This letter confirms the outcome of the meeting held on [Meeting date] about your role as [Job title]. You attended with [Companion].

## What happened

The concern discussed was [Issue]. At the meeting, [Employee explanation].

## Outcome

Having considered your explanation, I have decided to issue you with a [Warning level].

## What we expect

From now on, please [Expected standard].

## Support

To help, we will provide [Support offered].

## Review and duration

We will review progress over [Review period]. This warning will stay on your file and be taken into account for [Warning duration], after which it will be disregarded for disciplinary purposes.

If the required improvement is not made, or the issue happens again during that time, the next step may be [Next step].

## Your right to appeal

You may appeal against this warning by writing to [Appeal contact] by [Appeal deadline], explaining your reasons.

Yours sincerely,

[[signature:manager]]
