about: |
  Children travelling across borders without both parents, or with an adult who is not their parent, are sometimes asked for proof that the absent parent agrees. It is a safeguard against parental abduction and trafficking, and border officers and airlines can delay or refuse travel without it. A clear consent letter avoids that.

  This form identifies the child, the parent giving consent, and the adult travelling with them, with passport details for both, the destinations, dates and flights, and where the child will stay. It gives a contact number for the parent that officials can call, and authorises the accompanying adult to consent to emergency medical treatment, with any allergies or conditions noted.

  Requirements differ by country. Some ask for the consent to be notarised or to use a specific official form, and some require consent from every parent with parental responsibility. Check the rules of the countries the child is leaving and entering. A signed electronic copy is useful to carry, but bring a printed copy too.
