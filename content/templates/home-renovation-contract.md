about: |
  Renovations put a builder inside someone's home for weeks, and most disputes come from three places: a scope that was never quite agreed, changes made on a nod and invoiced later, and money paid ahead of the work. This contract addresses each one.

  It lists the work and the materials, including what the homeowner is choosing and supplying, says who obtains permits and books inspections, and ties each payment to a visible stage of progress rather than to dates. Any change, however small, needs a signed change order with its price and effect on the schedule before the work is done. A small final payment is held until the walkthrough list is finished, which keeps the last details from being left for months.

  It also sets out site rules that matter to people living in the house: working hours, protection of floors and furniture, daily clean-up, and how hidden problems, like rot behind a wall, are handled when they are found. Many places require specific consumer notices in home improvement contracts, such as a cancellation period; add those that apply.

# Home Renovation Contract

This contract is made on [Agreement date] between [Contractor name], [Contractor licence] (the "Contractor"), and [Homeowner name] (the "Homeowner"), for work at [Property address] (the "Property").

## 1. Scope of work

The Contractor will [Scope of work] (the "Work").

## 2. Materials

The Work uses [Materials]. Materials not available will be replaced only with equivalents the Homeowner approves in writing.

## 3. Permits and inspections

[Permits]. Work will not continue past any stage that requires an inspection until it has passed.

## 4. Schedule

Work starts on [Start date] and will be substantially complete by [Completion date]. The schedule moves for changes the Homeowner orders, hidden conditions, inspection delays, and events outside the Contractor's control, and the Contractor will tell the Homeowner promptly of any delay.

## 5. Price and payment

The contract price is [Contract price], paid as follows: [Payment stages]. Each payment is due within 5 days of the stage being reached and shown to the Homeowner.

## 6. Change orders

No change to the Work will be made without a written change order, signed by both parties, stating the change, its price and its effect on the schedule.

## 7. Hidden conditions

If the Contractor finds a condition that could not reasonably be seen before work started, such as rot, unsafe wiring or structural damage, it will stop work in that area, show the Homeowner and propose a change order. In a safety emergency it may make the area safe first.

## 8. Site rules

Work hours are [Work hours]. The Contractor will protect floors, furniture and fixtures in the work area, keep the site tidy, remove debris daily, and leave the Property secure at the end of each day.

## 9. Completion and walkthrough

At substantial completion, the parties will walk through the Property together and list any remaining items. The Contractor will finish them within 14 days, after which the final payment is due.

## 10. Warranty

The Contractor warrants its workmanship for [Warranty period] from completion. Manufacturers' warranties on materials and appliances are passed to the Homeowner.

## 11. Insurance

The Contractor will keep liability and workers' compensation insurance throughout the Work and provide certificates on request.
